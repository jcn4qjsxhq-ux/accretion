# Section 3.4: revised, insertion-ready text

To better understand the mechanisms underlying the eruptive behaviour of V346\,Nor, we fitted our multi-epoch data with the steady, optically thick and geometrically thin viscous accretion-disc model proposed for young eruptive stars by \citet{hk96} and first applied to V346\,Nor by \citet{kospal2017a}. Assuming a radially constant mass-accretion rate, the radial temperature profile is
\[
T_{\rm d}^{4}(R)=\frac{3GM_{\star}\dot{M}}{8\pi R^{3}\sigma}
\left[1-\left(\frac{R_{\star}}{R}\right)^{1/2}\right].
\]
The formal temperature reaches its maximum at $R=(49/36)R_{\star}\simeq1.36R_{\star}$; at smaller radii we set $T_{\rm d}(R)=T_{\rm max}$ to avoid the non-physical decline towards the zero-torque inner boundary. The disc SED was calculated by integrating the blackbody emission of concentric annuli between $R_{\rm in}$ and $R_{\rm out}$. The model fluxes were reddened using the extinction law of \citet{cardelli1989}, adopting $R_V=3.1$. Following \citet{kospal2017a}, we fixed $M_{\star}=0.5\,M_{\odot}$, $R_{\star}=R_{\rm in}=3\,R_{\odot}$, $R_{\rm out}=2$\,au, the disc inclination to $i=40^{\circ}$, and the distance to 700\,pc. The exact value of $R_{\rm out}$ has negligible influence on the near-infrared fluxes. With these assumptions, the two free parameters at each epoch are $M_{\star}\dot{M}$ and $A_V$. More precisely, the disc temperature and luminosity constrain the product $M_{\star}\dot{M}$; the conversion to $\dot M$ therefore scales inversely with the adopted stellar mass.

We experimented with fitting the photometric SEDs over several wavelength ranges. At some epochs the complete optical-to-infrared SED could be reproduced by the accretion-disc model, whereas at others clear optical and/or mid-infrared excess emission was present. We therefore adopted the $J$ and $H$ photometric measurements, at effective wavelengths of approximately 1.235 and 1.662\,$\mu$m, respectively. This choice gives somewhat lower values of $M_{\star}\dot M$ and $A_V$ than those reported by \citet{kospal2017a}, but reduces contamination by optical scattered light and by thermal emission from cooler dust at larger radii; in particular, the $K$ band often contains a substantial excess above the disc-only model. Each epoch was fitted independently (i.e. without temporal regularisation) by minimizing chi-squared in logarithmic flux density. A 5 per cent systematic uncertainty floor was added in quadrature to the measurement uncertainties. Because a $JH$ epoch contains two data points for two fitted parameters, these photometric solutions are model-dependent inversions rather than over-constrained fits and do not by themselves provide a goodness-of-fit test. Fits including $K$ were therefore retained as a sensitivity check, rather than adopted as the fiducial solution.

We also fitted the available near-infrared spectra to test the photometric results and the degeneracy between extinction and the assumed inner radius. For the direct $JH$ comparison, the IRTF/SpeX SXD spectra were fitted over 1.20--1.60\,$\mu$m, with the 1.30--1.40\,$\mu$m telluric interval masked. Separate IRTF $JHK$ fits over 1.20--2.20\,$\mu$m were used only as a sensitivity check and should not be confused with the fiducial $JH$ fit. The XSHOOTER $JH$ spectra were fitted over 1.15--1.75\,$\mu$m, after masking the unstable 1.34--1.48\,$\mu$m atmospheric band. The supplied $F_{\lambda}$ values were converted from erg\,s$^{-1}$\,cm$^{-2}$\,\AA$^{-1}$ to $F_{\nu}$ in Jy, robustly combined into 0.005\,$\mu$m bins, and tied in absolute level and spectral slope to contemporaneous $JH$ photometry. Because the XSHOOTER files contain no uncertainty spectra, the uncertainty of each bin combines its robust scatter with a 10 per cent calibration floor; the common 5 per cent systematic floor was then added by the fitter. The spectral fits broadly confirmed the $JH$ photometric results, but did not remove the covariance between $R_{\rm in}$ and $A_V$. We therefore retained the fixed value $R_{\rm in}=3\,R_{\odot}$ throughout. This degeneracy, together with possible deviations from a steady thin disc, should be kept in mind when interpreting the absolute parameter values; the relative temporal changes are more robust.

To investigate the optical and mid-infrared excesses, we subtracted the best-fitting accretion-disc spectrum from the measured SED, dereddened the residuals using the $A_V$ obtained for the disc, and fitted the optical and mid-infrared residuals separately with blackbody functions. These blackbodies are phenomenological descriptions of the excess components and are not part of the simultaneous $JH$ disc fit.

Figure~\ref{fig:accdisk} shows the resulting evolution of $M_{\star}\dot M$ and $A_V$. As discussed by \citet{kospal2020}, following its initial brightening in the late 1970s V346\,Nor maintained $M_{\star}\dot M\sim10^{-5}\,M_{\odot}^{2}\,{\rm yr}^{-1}$, rising to approximately $5\times10^{-5}\,M_{\odot}^{2}\,{\rm yr}^{-1}$ during the broad maximum between about 1990 and 2000. This was followed by a sharp decline to approximately $5\times10^{-8}\,M_{\odot}^{2}\,{\rm yr}^{-1}$ in 2010. By 2019 the source had gradually returned to its pre-fading state, while the fitted extinction also varied substantially. The accretion parameter subsequently increased to about $10^{-4}\,M_{\odot}^{2}\,{\rm yr}^{-1}$ in 2021.

In early 2023, V346\,Nor exhibited an unusual near-infrared colour change: it faded in $J$, remained approximately constant in $H$, and brightened slightly in $K$. Within the adopted model, the $JH$ behaviour requires a decrease of roughly two orders of magnitude in $M_{\star}\dot M$, accompanied by a fall in extinction to $A_V<5$\,mag. The simultaneous $K$-band brightening is not reproduced by the disc-only solution and instead demonstrates that the long-wavelength excess evolved independently. Later in 2023 the fitted parameters returned rapidly to high values. During 2024--2025, $M_{\star}\dot M$ was typically a few times $10^{-5}\,M_{\odot}^{2}\,{\rm yr}^{-1}$. The extinction declined from about 20\,mag in the second half of 2024 to a minimum of approximately 10\,mag in early 2025, while the accretion parameter changed much less. This temporary clearing can therefore account for a brightness maximum without requiring a comparable accretion burst. The connection to the Gaia alert should, however, be stated only if the alert date overlaps this extinction decline.

The 2026 measurements do not show a simple monotonic continuation of the earlier rise. The fiducial $JH$ fits give $M_{\star}\dot M\simeq8.3\times10^{-5}\,M_{\odot}^{2}\,{\rm yr}^{-1}$ and $A_V\simeq22.5$\,mag on 2026 May 4, followed by substantially lower values in early July and a partial recovery within three days. The corresponding $JHK$ solutions are systematically higher because of the $K$-band excess. Thus, the most robust conclusion is that V346\,Nor reached exceptionally high accretion states during 2021--2026, but both accretion and line-of-sight extinction remained strongly variable; the data do not support describing the full 2026 evolution as a steady increase.

Integrating the fiducial $JH$ accretion history by linear interpolation between all fitted epochs gives a total accreted mass of approximately $1.6\times10^{-3}\,M_{\odot}$ over the observed 47-yr interval for the adopted $M_{\star}=0.5\,M_{\odot}$. This estimate is sensitive to sparse temporal sampling: excluding gaps longer than one year yields approximately $5.8\times10^{-4}\,M_{\odot}$ over the directly covered intervals, while the $JHK$ sensitivity fit gives a larger value of approximately $2.9\times10^{-3}\,M_{\odot}$. The integrated mass should therefore be quoted as model-dependent and bracketed by the sampling and wavelength-selection systematics.
